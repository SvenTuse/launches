## Evidence and ordering rules

Views such as 228K or 5.7K are rounded by X; expanded integers are approximations, not exact analytics. Likes, reposts and replies are copied from visible counters. Bookmarks, private analytics and quote-post metrics inside cards are not included. Every account post is counted once; quoting its earlier post does not add the earlier post again. Third-party quoted copy is described under attachment and excluded from the account's totals.

The screenshots display September dates without years; **2026 is inferred from the profile's join month/year and the research context**. Calendar dates are sorted oldest to newest; within a day, the approximate bottom-to-top feed sequence is used. Exact same-day ordering is unknown without timestamps. The pinned launch is placed first for narrative readability. Two posts lack an absolute date and are kept in a separate final group; “6h” is an age at screenshot capture, not a time of day. Event hours mentioned in copy are not publication times.

“Show more” means the screenshot does not contain the rest of the text. The visible excerpt is reproduced and marked incomplete; missing wording is never reconstructed. Video descriptions are inferences from a still preview and copy. No video playback was available from local JPGs. Post claims, including payouts, burns, partnerships and reward values, remain claims unless independently established elsewhere in this research.

The strongest reach belongs to a short launch demo, geographic availability, the fee model and an incentive to post catch videos. Those five posts all exceed 100K views. Their engagement rates are below the captured mean, so reach alone does not prove strong intent or community conversion.

September 3–4 account for approximately 48.5% of captured views. This is accumulated exposure at screenshot time, not same-day impressions; older posts had longer to accrue it. The captured cadence is unusually dense on Sep 4 (14 posts): bigger drops, more cities, sponsor tools, scarcity events, payout proof, founder introduction and anti-cheat fixes all support the same product story. Later updates shift toward onboarding, Asia, leaderboards, user videos, social proof and operational repairs. Later lower views could reflect post age, fewer captured posts, topic choice or waning interest; this dataset cannot choose between them.

Creative consistency is strong: lime balloon lettering and floating company cubes are repeated over real streets, skylines and Earth. Image/video assets usually communicate one item—102 cities, 75% fees, 1M burned, a particular city, or one share. Literal outdoor demos add credibility to stylized renders. The Sep 14 3x-sharing post has 108K views but only 242 visible interactions, so there is evidence of distribution, not proof of how many user videos or new users it produced.

The Sep 22 poll has 132 votes. Retail/restaurants (37.1%) and live sports/events (32.6%) lead, but this is a small self-selected audience. Sep 23's mall example shows a direct feedback-to-post loop. Bot-cleanup copy, failed claims and chart-FUD replies show friction alongside adoption; they should remain in the case study.
