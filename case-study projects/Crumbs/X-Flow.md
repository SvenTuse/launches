## Posting structure and marketing interpretation

The prelaunch video P01 supplies 59.29% of captured views and the launch video P03 another 25.54%; together they contribute **84.83%**. Their view counts are approximately **1.2M** and **517K**, compared with **12K–50K** for the other official captures. This establishes concentration of attention in two explainer hooks, not causation or ad spending.

The sequence repeatedly turns a familiar behavior into a concrete reward: Costco → COST, Apple → AAPL, Netflix → NFLX, Amazon → AMZN. A short consumer promise brings people in, mobile/notification and review-interface creatives visualize the experience, numerical usage and burn announcements build social proof, and brief CA/link replies reduce navigation friction. P04’s 43K-view dog joke also turns a complaint into an entertaining explanation of what counts as a receipt.

White/mint/lime surfaces, dark-green rounded lowercase typography, the pixel C, translucent coins and recurring mosaic end cards create a recognizable social identity. Merchant-logo tiles are exceptionally easy to recognize, but recognition is not evidence of brand participation. Ordinary X feed posts are the evidenced channel; paid ads, paid KOL placements and incentivized creator posts are **not established** by these screenshots.

Usage numbers are self-reported: “over 8,500 receipt submissions” in P06 and “reviewing over 1,500” in P10 could describe total submissions versus a pending queue, but this interpretation is unverified. The 3% burn announcement and transaction hash require chain verification before calling them executed fee-funded buybacks. High reach and attractive renders establish attention; they do not establish fulfilled rewards, retained users or project profitability.
