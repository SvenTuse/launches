# legs.fun — website analysis

Research date: **27 September 2026 (Europe/Moscow)**. Website supplied in project metadata: [https://legs.fun/](https://legs.fun/).

## 1. Overall essence, product and narrative

The advertised product is an **on-chain short-term parlay application**: select several memecoin and/or tokenized-stock markets, set a timer, and receive the ticket's combined multiplier only when every selected leg meets its target. It gives the familiar sports-betting parlay a market-price subject. “LEGS” is both the betting unit and a rising-market metaphor, allowing the product and speculative message to share a name.

This is described as an application rather than only a promotional landing page. The [primary launch thread](https://x.com/legsdotfun/status/2095619171002593725) describes ticket creation and a vault; the supplied [Stitch commentary](x-ca-mentions/info.md) repeats that story. However, the actual application could not be opened during this research, so this classification reflects the advertised product, not a successful live workflow test.

### Live inspection and recovery limits

- **First browser visit:** September 27, 2026, **13:30:29 UTC / 16:30:29 Moscow**. Cloudflare displayed **“Web server is down — Error code 521.”**
- **Second browser check:** **14:00:15 UTC / 17:00:15 Moscow**, after reloading the root URL; the same 521 condition remained.
- The browser and Cloudflare were marked working; the legs.fun host was marked error. The visible page states the origin server was not returning a connection.

A point-in-time host failure does not establish when the application stopped working or whether it worked at launch. It prevents verification of the original web interface, current supported markets and end-to-end transactions.

## 2. Technical functionality, with evidence status

### Advertised ticket flow

1. **Market selection:** combine **3–10 markets**; memecoins, tokenized stocks or both. [Primary market/time reply](https://x.com/legsdotfun/status/2095619174320009388).
2. **Duration selection:** **5, 15 or 60 minutes**. Targets are described as adjusted for each market's typical movement; no formula, volatility estimate or calibration data was recovered.
3. **Multiplier selection:** farther targets correspond to higher selected multipliers. The project's examples multiply five 2× legs into **32×** and five 4× legs into **1,024×**. [Primary multiplier reply](https://x.com/legsdotfun/status/2095619177092423932).
4. **Hit logic:** touching the price target is sufficient; staying above it at expiry is not required. Every leg must hit before time ends. [Primary settlement reply](https://x.com/legsdotfun/status/2095619180644966656).
5. **Outcome:** all legs hit → advertised combined payout; one misses → the ticket fails. This is a product rule, not an independently executed settlement.
6. **Funding/counterparty:** the Legs vault is described as retaining lost stakes and paying winning tickets, with holders able to use LEGS for tickets or deposit into the vault. [Primary vault reply](https://x.com/legsdotfun/status/2095619185812344987).
7. **Withdrawals:** P08 says withdrawals from closed tickets would be enabled once an updated deployment completed, with temporary latency expected to resolve in five minutes. It announces a rollout, not proof of completed withdrawals.
8. **Holder incentive:** P05 advertises double rewards for six hours for LEGS holders. Reward amount, accounting, eligibility threshold and actual distribution were not recovered.

### Claimed token economics

M24 says **80% of all fees generated by Legs** are allocated to LEGS buybacks and burns; the primary vault reply frames the 80% as **protocol revenue generated by play** used for open-market purchases. The wording does not establish whether “fees” is gross revenue, net revenue, a trading tax or another base; it should not be modelled without contract/accounting evidence.

M19 claims **over 3% of supply burned during the first hour**. The captured creative is a claim, not a transaction receipt. The research recovered neither a complete burn-address series nor fee-to-buyback routing, ticket volume, house profit, reserve solvency or a functioning vault balance.

The announced **$10M in LEGS trading volume in three hours** concerns trading in the token, not demonstrated ticket stakes or platform revenue. There is no verified project profit figure.

### Technical presence versus implementation

The exact supplied CA resolves to a [Robinhood Chain Blockscout token contract named legs.fun / LEGS](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x8fcf98e1348d3ddee46cdd15a5c7d9a8d423077d). This supports token identity and chain presence, not the ticket builder's implementation. No verified app-contract addresses, source repository, independent audit, price-oracle documentation, dispute process, target-calculation formula, payout caps, liquidity lock, reserve dashboard or fee-accounting dashboard was recovered.

Robinhood's [own chain documentation](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/?lang=en) describes an open EVM-compatible network and says ecosystem inclusion is not endorsement. The bio's “Bootstrapped by @RobinhoodCrypto” remains unconfirmed; it is not evidence of a Robinhood brokerage listing.

No wallet was connected and no ticket, deposit, approval, withdrawal or trade was submitted. This research is a read-only inspection.

## 3. Design, interface and branding

### What is observable on the live site

The only current interface is a **standard Cloudflare diagnostic page**: white/light-grey background, large thin grey error heading, small explanatory body copy, three status columns, green browser/Cloudflare indicators and a red host-error indicator, blue help links, and a footer. The help and hidden-IP-reveal controls belong to Cloudflare; they are not legs.fun product design. No legs.fun animation can be observed on this page.

Consequently, the original page's menu placement, button styling, responsive layout, wallet connection location, ticket-builder panels, motion, loading states, accessibility and vault screens are **unavailable**. Describing them as inspected would invent evidence.

### Recovered project visual identity

The supplied social images and link previews still establish a detailed brand system:

- **Primary color:** luminous yellow-green/lime, used for the letter L, LEGS wordmark, GM greeting and numerical announcements.
- **Secondary setting:** saturated sky blue, clouds, pale horizon, green fields and photorealistic travel/outdoor backgrounds.
- **Typography:** oversized glossy 3D balloon lettering for the brand and milestone numerals; small, light sans-serif white captions for messages; occasional subtly spaced “LEGS FUN” labels. No exact font family is identifiable.
- **Recurring object:** inflated L or the full LEGS wordmark; it persists in the avatar, profile banner, market panels, sky scenes, field, blimp and numeral cards.
- **Image composition:** one exaggerated subject dominates; supporting people, aircraft windows or a paraglider provide scale/context. These are surreal composited renders; the production tool is unknown.
- **Film format:** a 49-second city launch video with a giant L in a building scene and a person beside a car; an 11-second beach reaction clip. The screenshot previews support scenes and duration, not a verified account of the entire film or its audio.
- **Website share preview:** the root-domain card repeatedly shows monochrome lime inflated LEGS lettering and a small legs.fun badge. This supports continuity between social creatives and the website's **share metadata image**, not the original website's whole layout.
- **Market/profile continuity:** multiple third-party chart posts reproduce the same airplane-window banner and L avatar, keeping the brand visible at the point where viewers inspect or trade the token.

### Branding conclusion

The strongest visible design choice is the **inflated lime letter system**, not a recovered application layout. It makes the campaign legible in small avatars, large cinematic scenes and numerical milestone cards, while skies/flight imagery gives “next leg up” a repeatable visual meaning. Pairing that same identity with contract and market cards likely reduced recognition friction; attributable click or purchase uplift was not measured.

The account campaign, website-preview art and trading surfaces are demonstrably consistent. Whether the actual website shared the same colors, animations and visual hierarchy cannot be confirmed while its origin is unavailable.

## 4. What a launcher can learn from this website evidence

- Build a product whose core action fits a single sentence and whose name generates both a mechanic and a creative metaphor.
- Treat the domain preview image as a useful distribution surface: it appeared repeatedly even in address-only replies.
- Put usable flows and auditable settlement/revenue evidence behind promotional numbers; the rollout post and skeptical replies show why that matters.
- Preserve launch screenshots and product documentation. Here, loss of the original site makes historical functionality much harder to substantiate.
